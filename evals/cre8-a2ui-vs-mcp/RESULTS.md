## What these numbers do not measure

Every freecode arm scores 1.000, and that is a limit of the scorer rather than
a finding about the arms. The deliverable there is React that has to compile,
so TypeScript and the installed type declarations already reject the invalid
components, props, enums and slots these dimensions exist to catch. The
dimensions are saturated; they cannot separate the arms.

The arms are separated, visibly and systematically, on things nothing here
scores. Averaged over three trials each:

| arm | nodes | distinct components | slots used | rendered height |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| baseline-freecode | 306 | 44.0 | 23.0 | 4.7-6.2k px |
| cre8-mcp-freecode | 307 | 48.0 | 30.7 | 4.8-5.8k px |
| cre8-mcp-design-freecode | 215 | 37.3 | 16.7 | 3.4-4.1k px |

The MCP arm used six components baseline never once reached for across three
trials - Cre8Form, Cre8Field, Cre8Layout, Cre8LayoutSection, Cre8Feature,
Cre8Popover - and placed 33% more content into named slots at the same node
count. It builds the same volume out of more of the library. The arm with the
design skill builds roughly a third less and reads as more disciplined.

Whether "more of the library" or "less, more deliberately" is better is a
design judgment, and this eval cannot make it. Read the freecode row as
"no arm produced invalid markup", not as "the arms are equivalent".

The MCP arm's six violations are cre8-field.errorText and .successText, both
inert - reached only because it was the arm that discovered Cre8Field at all.
Reaching further into the library exposes more inert-prop traps; that argues
for fixing the props, not for reaching less.
